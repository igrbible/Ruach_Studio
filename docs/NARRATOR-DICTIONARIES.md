# The Narrator's stress dictionaries

The Narrator puts a stress mark on each word before the engine reads it, because a voice model guesses stresses and often guesses wrong («стука́ють» read as «стукаю́ть»). This guide says where the marks come from, which dictionaries come with the Studio and from whom, and how to plug in a large dictionary of your own for any language.

## Where a word's stress comes from

For every word of a text, in this order:

1. **Your own mark.** A word you wrote with an acute (U+0301, «моло́ко») or, in Russian and Belarusian, with ё is left as you wrote it. Always.
2. **Your own small dictionary** of that language, edited in the room (Stress dictionary). It overrides everything below.
3. **The language's big dictionary**, when one is here: a word form with one stress gets it; a form the dictionary stresses two ways (за́мок / замо́к) is a homograph, left as it is and named in Check, so you pick its stress in place. The Narrator never guesses a homograph.
4. A word of one vowel needs no mark. A word found nowhere is read as written and counted («not found»).

Check (beside Read) shows the text exactly as the engine will read it, with every mark, the homographs and the words found nowhere.

## The dictionaries that come with the Studio

**Ukrainian.** Its stresses are those of «Словники України» (Dictionaries of Ukraine) of the Ukrainian Lingua-Information Fund of the National Academy of Sciences of Ukraine (ULIF, https://lcorp.ulif.org.ua/dictua/, © ULIF). lang-uk (https://lang.org.ua) made them the stress dictionary of its package ukrainian-word-stress (https://github.com/lang-uk/ukrainian-word-stress; the dictionary generated by Oleksiy Syvokon), published under the MIT License. The Studio's index was built from that package's version 2.1.0 (2,892,732 word forms, 42,481 of them homographs) and is shared under the same MIT License, with a `NOTICE` that names every link. It comes with the Narrator: Engine → Models → Narrator, or `bash tools/narrator-setup.sh`, puts it in `models/narrator/dicts/uk/`.

**Russian** (from 2.0.8). Its stresses are those of A. A. Zaliznyak's «Грамматический словарь русского языка», 6th edition, 2010; the exclusive rights to it belong to Anna A. Zaliznyak, and the project gramdict (https://gramdict.ru, https://github.com/gramdict/zalizniak-2010) publishes its proofs as data with her consent under CC BY-NC 4.0. That data gives each headword with Zaliznyak's index codes («жо 1*d, ё»), not every form, so the Studio unfolded it: every article into all its forms with their stresses, by Zaliznyak's own declension and conjugation tables (2,249,271 word forms from 107,522 of the 109,866 articles; the other articles give their headword and the forms they write out; prepositions, conjunctions and particles are left out, since their stress belongs to the phrase). Measured against a full accented paradigm made from Zaliznyak by others, 99.1 % of the forms both stress one way agree. The index is shared under the same CC BY-NC 4.0, with a `NOTICE` that names every link and the licence's text beside it: **free for non-commercial use; commercial use needs a licence from gramdict (human@gramdict.ru)**. It comes with the Narrator into `models/narrator/dicts/ru/`. A StarDict of the «full accented paradigm» kind, when you have one, still goes in as it is (see «A StarDict») and is read before it.

**Belarusian** (from 2.0.8). Its stresses are those of the Grammar Database of the Belarusian Language (GrammarDB) of the Belarusian NLP community (https://github.com/Belarus/GrammarDB, the web interface at https://bnkorpus.info/grammar.be.html), built from the major dictionaries of Belarusian, among them «Вялікі слоўнік беларускай мовы: арфаграфія, акцэнтуацыя, парадыгматыка» (2012) and the grammatical dictionaries of 2008 and 2013. Its verified release (RELEASE-202601) lists every word form with its stress (1,292,710 lines, 1,259,997 forms; 16,538 stressed two ways, named and never guessed); the Studio turned the list into its index as it is, and a word written with ў after a vowel (яго́ ўла́сных) is looked up by its у. Measured on the testimony's Belarusian text against the stresses set there by hand: 286 of its 303 words of two vowels or more known, 236 of the 244 known one way agree. GrammarDB is shared under CC BY-SA 4.0, and so is the index, with a `NOTICE` and the licence's text beside it: **free to share and adapt, also commercially, with the attribution kept and adaptations under the same licence**. It comes with the Narrator into `models/narrator/dicts/be/`.

**Greek** needs no dictionary: it writes its stress in its own spelling. Polytonic text (Ἐν ἀρχῇ ἦν ὁ λόγος) goes to the engine monotonic (Εν αρχή ην ο λόγος): read aloud today the two sound alike, the breathings and the iota subscript are silent and the acute, the grave and the circumflex all mean the stress, while the engines learnt from monotonic Greek. The lunate sigma (ϲ) becomes σ or ς. Your text in the editor is not changed, only what the engine reads; an engine's `engine.json` may say `"el_monotonic": false` to read polytonic as it is.

**Other languages.** None yet. Your own small dictionary works for every language from the first day; a big one goes in as below.

## A dictionary of your own, in the format of ours

The Narrator reads a language's big dictionary from one file:

```
models/narrator/dicts/LANG/forms.sqlite
```

where `LANG` is the language's code as the room lists it (`ru`, `uk`, `be`, `el`, `es`, `de`…). You never write that file by hand: you give the builder a plain list, and it writes the file.

### 1. The list

One word form a line, UTF-8, with its stressed vowel marked:

```
# Belarusian, my own list (a line starting with # is a comment)
малако́
за́мак
зама́к
Ві́льня	name
вільня́
п+оле
```

- The stress is an acute after the vowel (U+0301: `малако́`) or a `+` before it (`п+оле`). Both become the acute.
- **Every form you want stressed is its own line**: `книга`, `книги`, `книге`, `книгу`… The Narrator looks words up as they stand in the text, it does not decline or conjugate. A dictionary of headwords only stresses headwords.
- A form written twice with different stresses (`за́мак`, `зама́к`) is a **homograph**: both are kept, and the Narrator names it instead of choosing.
- A second field after a Tab saying `name` marks a proper name (`Ві́льня	name`). Inside a sentence, a word written with a capital is looked up among the names first, so a city is not read as a common word; at a sentence's start the common word's forms are used, since a capital there says nothing. Any other second field is ignored, so a list with notes or tags after the Tab works as it is.
- A line without any stress mark is skipped and counted. A line that is not a word (digits, punctuation alone) is skipped and counted.
- Letters a language writes with an accent as one character (Spanish `ó`, Greek `ά`) are letters of the language, not stress marks: they are kept as they are, and the stress mark is still the separate acute.
- `.csv` works too: the first comma separates the form from the rest of the line.

### 2. The build

From the Studio's folder:

```
.venv/bin/python lab/reader_dict_build.py be my-belarusian.tsv --source "where it is from" --licence "its licence"
```

It writes `models/narrator/dicts/be/forms.sqlite` and says how many words, homographs and names it found, and how many lines it skipped. `--out FILE` writes elsewhere. Give `--source` and `--licence`: they are kept inside the index (its table `about`), so whoever you share it with knows what it may carry.

Then restart the lab, or the whole Studio, so the Narrator opens it. Check in the Narrator's room now says the dictionary is the language's own (`own:be`).

### 3. A StarDict

A StarDict whose articles list a headword's stressed forms separated by commas (the Russian «full accented paradigm» is one) is built the same way, from its `.idx` (the `.dict.dz` or `.dict` beside it):

```
.venv/bin/python lab/reader_dict_build.py ru paradigm.idx --source "…" --licence "…"
```

For Russian there is a second way: put the StarDict's three files (`.ifo`, `.idx`, `.dict.dz`) into `models/narrator/dicts/ru/zaliznyak/`, and the lab indexes it itself at its start (`forms.sqlite` beside them, built once, rebuilt when the StarDict is newer). While it builds, a reading goes without it and its report says so.

### 4. What the index holds

For those who want to read or make one another way:

| table | columns | what |
|---|---|---|
| `forms` | `k` TEXT PRIMARY KEY, `f` TEXT | the key: the form in lower case, without stress marks, ё as е, the apostrophe as `'`; its stressed forms in lower case, joined by `\|` (two or more: a homograph) |
| `cap` | `k`, `f` (optional) | the same for proper names, looked up first for a capitalized word inside a sentence |
| `about` | `k`, `v` (optional) | its language, source, licence, the date and the counts |

The Ukrainian index that comes with the Studio has exactly this shape.

### 5. A test

Before you trust it with a whole book:

1. Write five lines in the room: words you know, a homograph, a name inside a sentence and at a sentence's start.
2. Press Check: every mark is shown, the homograph is named, the name is stressed only inside the sentence.
3. Read the five lines and listen. If a word is still read wrong, put it into your own small dictionary: it wins over the big one.

## Order and sharing

Ukrainian: a list you build for `uk` takes the place of the Studio's index (the same file). Russian: Zaliznyak's StarDict folder, when it is here, is read before the Studio's Russian index or a `ru` list you built (which takes the index's place, the same file). A proper name (a form marked `name`) is looked up only for a word with a capital: inside a sentence the names first, at a sentence's start the names beside the common words, two stresses between them a homograph.

A dictionary is someone's work. Before you share an index you built, check that its source lets you: the builder keeps what you said about it, but it cannot know whether that is true.
